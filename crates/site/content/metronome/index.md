grooph is a free metronome that runs in your browser. There is nothing to install and no
account to create. Pick a tempo, pick a time signature and press play. The first beat of
every measure sounds higher than the others, so you always know where "one" is.

```open | Open the metronome at 100 BPM in 4/4
bpm=100&ts=4/4
```

## How to use it

1. **Set the tempo.** Tap **BPM** in the card at the top and use the buttons, the slider or
   **Tap** to tap it in. Any tempo from 20 to 300 beats per minute works.
2. **Choose a time signature.** Open the **Edit** tab and use the time signature
   button (it shows the current meter, e.g. "4/4"). You can use 1 to 7 beats over a quarter,
   3 to 13 over an eighth or 5 to 19 over a sixteenth note, so 3/4, 6/8, 7/8 and 12/8 are
   all possible.
3. **Press play** (or the space bar). A moving cursor shows where you are in the measure.
4. **Optional:** switch on **Count-in** on the **Practice** tab to hear one bar of clicks
   before the music starts, or add a feel under **Swing**.

On phones and tablets the screen stays awake while the metronome is playing.

## More than a click

A plain click only marks the beats. grooph can also play a **rhythm**: write notes and
rests in standard notation, including triplets and other tuplets, and the metronome plays
exactly that pattern. This is useful when you want to hear how a figure from your sheet
music should sound.

```rhythm bpm=100 | Son clave (3-2)
4/4 q. q. q | r:q q q r:q
```

If you would rather read something new every time, the
[rhythm generator](/rhythm-generator/) writes random exercises for sight-reading.

## Tips for practicing with a metronome

- **Start slow.** Pick a tempo at which you can play the passage without mistakes, then
  raise it in small steps of 4 to 8 BPM.
- **Count out loud.** The [counting guides](/learn/) show how to count eighths, sixteenths
  and triplets.
- **Turn the click off in your head, not in the app.** Try to "hide" the click under your
  own notes. If you hear it clearly, you are early or late.
- **Use a half-time click** for fast pieces: set the metronome to half the tempo and feel
  each click as beats one and three.
- **Let the metronome drop out or speed up.** The [metronome trainer](/metronome/trainer/)
  mutes bars so you keep the time yourself, or raises the tempo step by step.

## FAQ

### Is the metronome free?

Yes. grooph is free to use in the browser, without an account or ads.

### Does it work on my phone?

Yes. grooph runs in current mobile browsers. Tap play once to allow sound; some phones
mute web audio while the ringer switch is set to silent.

### Can I change the click sound?

Yes. By default the metronome plays claves: a high hit on the downbeat and a lower one on
the other main beats, and the rhythm plays a snare drum with its own recordings for
normal notes, accents and ghost notes. Under Settings → Sounds you can switch the
metronome or the rhythm to a plain click, whose waveform you can change. The mixer
(speaker icon) has two groups: the metronome (downbeat, beats, soft clicks, count-in) and
the rhythm (notes, accents, ghost notes). Each group can be muted, for example to hear
only the written rhythm. What the metronome clicks is set per time signature in the
Metronome tab under **Clicks**: **Beats** gives 7/8 three clicks per bar, **Eighths** all
seven, with the eighths between the beats played soft.

The claves samples are by [altemark](https://freesound.org/people/altemark/packs/11638/)
on freesound.org, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial (CC BY-NC).

### Can it keep a steady pulse for odd meters?

Yes. Meters from 1/4 to 7/4, 3/8 to 13/8 and 5/16 to 19/16 work, for example 5/4, 7/8
or 11/16.
See [odd time signatures](/metronome/odd-time-signatures/).
