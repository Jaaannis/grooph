In jazz, blues, shuffle and many hip-hop grooves, pairs of eighth notes are not played
evenly. The first note of each pair is longer, the second one shorter and later. This is
called **swing**. grooph can swing any rhythm while the notation stays straight, exactly
as swung music is usually written.

```open | Open a swing click at 120 BPM (triplet feel)
bpm=120&ts=4/4&swing=66
```

## How much swing?

grooph describes swing as the share of the pair taken by the first note:

| Setting | Feel | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | straight, no swing | 1:1 |
| 58–60% | light swing, common at fast tempos | about 3:2 |
| 66% | triplet swing, the classic jazz and shuffle feel | 2:1 |
| 75% | dotted, a hard shuffle | 3:1 |

Set the amount under **Swing** on the **Practice** tab. You can swing eighth notes
(jazz, blues, shuffle) or sixteenth notes (funk, hip-hop, neo-soul).

## Hear the difference

The same written eighths, first straight, then swung:

```rhythm bpm=110 | Straight eighths
4/4 e e e e e e e e
```

```rhythm bpm=110 swing=66 | The same eighths with 66% swing
4/4 e e e e e e e e
```

Swing is most obvious on figures with offbeats:

```rhythm bpm=120 swing=66 | A swung comping figure
4/4 q r:e e r:e e q
```

```rhythm bpm=90 swing=58 su=16 | Sixteenth-note swing for a funk groove
4/4 e s s s s e e s s q
```

## Tips

- **Swing depends on tempo.** At slow tempos the triplet feel (66%) sounds natural; at
  fast tempos most players swing much less.
- **Triplets stay triplets.** grooph plays written triplets as written, also when swing is
  on – just like a band would.
- **Feel it on two and four.** Turn the metronome down in your head and imagine the
  hi-hat closing on beats two and four.

## FAQ

### Why is swung music written in straight eighths?

Writing every swung pair as a triplet would be hard to read. Musicians agree to read
straight eighths and play them swung when the piece says "swing".

### Is 66% swing the same as triplets?

Yes. At 66% the first note takes two thirds of the beat and the second one third, which
is the rhythm of a quarter-and-eighth triplet.
